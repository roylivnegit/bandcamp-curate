**Proposals**


**Make the order of the feed mean something**

- _title_: Make the order of the feed mean something
- _problem_: The records at the top of Roy's feed are there because they carry a genre label that is common in his collection, not because people whose taste matches his bought them. He already owns plenty of techno — being told about more techno is not a discovery. And anything a single stranger owns is in the list at all, which is why there are ~1,600 of them and nobody scrolls past the first screen.
- _proposal_: Three changes in `backend/app/curation/engine.py`, one commit: (1) normalise tag affinity to a 0–1 share of Roy's collection instead of a raw count, so it can only break ties as the comment at :43 already promises; (2) weight each co-owner by how much their collection actually overlaps Roy's, divided by the size of their collection, so a 200-record collector who shares 40% counts for more than an 8,000-record hoarder who co-owns everything with everyone; (3) require at least 2 weighted co-owners for an album to enter the feed at all. Ships with a small synthetic corpus in tests covering a mega-supporter, a tight-overlap collector, and a heavily-tagged album with one owner — which is the start of the seed fixture E0-2 needs anyway.
- _why now_: It is the only product change on the board that is fully buildable and testable without the sandbox, without Neon and without a single Nimble credit — the whole thing lives in one scoring function and one test corpus. Everything else on the backlog needs infrastructure we do not have yet.
- _surface_: curation
- _effort_: M
- _measure_: Feed size drops from ~1,600 to something under 200. In the synthetic corpus, an album owned only by a mega-supporter ranks below one owned by two tight-overlap collectors, and a heavily-tagged single-owner album no longer outranks a genuine co-ownership hit. The real check is Roy reading the top 20 and saying how many he would click — record that number in metrics.md as the first honest datapoint.
- _kill criteria_: If, on the synthetic corpus, overlap-weighting produces the same order as the plain count, the mega-supporter effect is imaginary and we drop parts (2) and keep the cheap normalisation. If the co-owner floor empties the feed rather than trimming it, the graph is too sparse and the real problem is crawl coverage, not scoring — write that down and go there instead.
- _evidence_:
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:43 — comment claims co-ownership dominates and tag affinity breaks ties
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:386 — score = 1.0*co_owners + 0.25*tag_affinity, where co_owners is a small integer
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:198-209 — tag_affinity is an unbounded raw count of Roy's albums per tag; one popular tag can contribute 50+ points
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:355-372 — no minimum co-owner threshold; every album any neighbour owns is a candidate
    - team/memory/backlog.md — 'Why is the feed 1,600 items?' and 'Mega-supporters flatten the signal'
    - CLAUDE.md M4 — 'curate → 1,600 recs (= 1,600 distinct bands)'

**Say who owns it, not how many**

- _title_: Say who owns it, not how many
- _problem_: A recommendation arrives as a number. Roy has no way to judge whether to spend three minutes listening. 'Owned by 4 collectors' means nothing; 'owned by 4 collectors who each share about a third of your collection, one of whom owns every record on your favourite label' is a reason he can act on.
- _proposal_: Put the overlap numbers already implied by the graph into `reasons` and render them as a sentence in the React feed row: how many neighbours, how closely their taste matches, and which of Roy's own records led here (`reasons.seed_tags` already carries the last part and the UI shows a thin 'via …').
- _why now_: Cheap on its own, but it is the natural second half of the scoring item — computing per-neighbour overlap is what makes both possible. If the Lead wants a smaller cycle than the scoring change, this is the version that still moves the goal.
- _surface_: frontend
- _effort_: S
- _measure_: Every row in the top 20 carries a sentence with at least one concrete number or a named record of Roy's. Roy reads them and says which he believes.
- _kill criteria_: If the overlap numbers turn out to be uniformly low across all neighbours, the sentence is noise dressed as evidence and we say so rather than shipping a confident-sounding lie.
- _evidence_:
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:394-399 — reasons = co_owners, tag_affinity, matched_tags, seed_tags; all counts, no relationships
    - team/memory/backlog.md — 'Explanations Roy can trust'
    - charter.md 'What good looks like' #2

### Round 2

**[architect]** — _amend_

I am changing my ask on part (2): drop the collection-size denominator entirely, because it
cannot be made correct here. Any denominator big enough to punish a hoarder has to tell 400
items apart from 8,000, and that is exactly the band PAGES_PER_VISIT=10 makes
indistinguishable — a parked mega-supporter and a finished 400-record collector both show
about 400 fan_items. min(n,K) makes truncation harmless but hands the hoarder the smaller
denominator, so it removes the product effect; max(n,K) keeps the effect and keeps the
inversion. Use inverse-fan-frequency instead: weight a co-owner by overlap_with_roy / |roy's
collection| times 1/log2(2 + contributions(f)), where contributions(f) is how many of this
scan's candidates that fan co-owns — both terms move the same way under partial crawl (a
half-ingested neighbour loses overlap and loses reach together, so they can only be under-
weighted, never promoted), the first denominator is the one count the system guarantees
complete, and contributions is a second GROUP BY over the exact predicate at engine.py:365 —
no frontier read, no new coupling between curation and crawl state. It also targets the real
complaint: "co-owns everything with everyone" is a breadth problem, not a size problem.
Separate defect for the same commit: the track loop at engine.py:434 has no tag term at all,
so normalising tag affinity to 0–1 silently reweights albums against tracks, and with
one_per_band=True that decides which item represents a band for a reason unrelated to taste.

- _decision_: build

- _why_: The feed's top rows are ordered by an unbounded raw tag count (W_TAG_AFFINITY=0.25 times a number that can exceed 50, engine.py:386) against a co-owner count that is a small integer — so the comment at engine.py:43 promising that co-ownership dominates and tags only break ties is not what the code does.
