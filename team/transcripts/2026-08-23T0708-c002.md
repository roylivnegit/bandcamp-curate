## 2 · Grooming

**Proposals**


**The top of the feed is ranked by the wrong thing**

- _title_: The top of the feed is ranked by the wrong thing
- _problem_: Roy opens the feed and the first records he sees are not the best ones. They are the ones tagged with his most common genres, and often only one other person owns them. He has to scroll past those to find anything worth hearing, so he stops scrolling.
- _proposal_: Two changes to compute_recommendations, shipped together.

1. Put the two score terms on the same scale. tag_affinity is currently a raw count over Roy's whole collection, so it swamps co_owners. Normalise it to 0..1 — divide each tag's weight by the count of his most common tag, and average over the candidate's tags rather than summing, so a record with five generic tags does not beat one with two exact ones. Then the declared weights (1.0 / 0.25) mean what the comment says they mean.

2. Add a co-ownership floor. Drop candidates below N neighbours (start at 2, make it a config field so we can tune it without a migration). One person owning a record is noise.

Ship with a scoring-distribution script that prints, for a corpus: the number of candidates, the share at each co_owners value, and what fraction of the median score comes from the tag term. That script is how we measure this one and the next one.
- _why now_: It is the cheapest explanation we have for the two symptoms the charter names — 1,600 recs, and precision over volume. It is a change to arithmetic in one function, not to the crawl or the schema, so it is reviewable in one sitting. And every later ranking idea (neighbour weighting, better tag sources, a second data source) is measured against a baseline that is currently mis-scaled. Fix the ruler before measuring anything with it.
- _surface_: curation
- _effort_: M
- _measure_: Overlap between the current top 50 and the new top 50 — a big change means the bug was real. Share of the feed with co_owners == 1 (expect the 1,600 to drop sharply). Share of the median score contributed by the tag term (should go from near-total to roughly the 20% the weights imply). Then the one that matters: Roy reads the new top 20 and says how many he would click.
- _kill criteria_: If, on a real corpus, the current top 50 and a top 50 ranked by co_owners alone overlap by more than 80%, then the scale problem is not actually changing what Roy sees and this is theory. Drop it and go straight to neighbour weighting. Also drop the floor half if raising it to 2 removes more than about 95% of the feed — that would mean the co-ownership signal is too thin to threshold and the real problem is crawl coverage.
- _evidence_:
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:45-46 — W_CO_OWNER = 1.0, W_TAG_AFFINITY = 0.25, with the comment 'co-ownership dominates; tag affinity breaks ties'
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:198-208 — _my_tag_profile returns a raw count of owned albums per tag, unnormalised
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:384-386 — tag_affinity = sum(tag_profile[t] for t in tags); score = 1.0*co_owners + 0.25*tag_affinity. Collection is ~1,700 albums / 35,156 album_tags (CLAUDE.md M6 migration counts), so the tag term reaches the hundreds while co_owners stays in single digits.
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:369-386 — album candidates are grouped with no HAVING, so co_owners == 1 is a recommendation
    - CLAUDE.md M4 — 'curate → 1,600 recs (= 1,600 distinct bands)'
    - CLAUDE.md M4 — 'Tag-affinity/genre-filter data grows as album pages are crawled', i.e. coverage is sparse, which is what lets the few tagged candidates dominate

**A collector who owns everything tells us nothing**

- _title_: A collector who owns everything tells us nothing
- _problem_: Some collectors on Bandcamp own thousands of records. They co-own something with everyone, so they push records into Roy's feed just by being prolific. The person whose taste actually matches his — 200 records, most of which he also owns — counts exactly the same.
- _proposal_: Stop counting neighbours and start weighting them. Give each neighbour a weight before summing: how much of their collection overlaps Roy's, divided by the size of their collection (so breadth is discounted). Then co_owners becomes a weighted sum, and the reason string can say something real — 'owned by 7 collectors who share 40% of your taste' instead of 'owned by 7 collectors'. That is charter item 2 falling out of charter item 1 for free.

Measure before building. First pass is a query, not a feature: what does the distribution of neighbour collection sizes actually look like?
- _why now_: It was already on the backlog with 'worth measuring before building' attached, and the distribution script in the first proposal is exactly that measurement. If we build that script anyway, this becomes cheap to decide rather than cheap to argue about. But it should follow the scale fix, not precede it — weighting a term that is already swamped by the tag term changes nothing Roy sees.
- _surface_: curation
- _effort_: M
- _measure_: Distribution of neighbour collection sizes (p50, p90, max) — that alone tells us whether mega-supporters exist here. Then: how much the top 50 moves, and whether the records that leave are ones Roy recognises as generic.
- _kill criteria_: If neighbour collection sizes are tight — say p90 divided by p50 under 3 — there are no mega-supporters in this graph and the weighting has nothing to correct. Write that in tried-and-failed and move on.
- _evidence_:
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:365 — func.count(func.distinct(FanItem.fan_id)) — every neighbour counts exactly 1, regardless of collection size or overlap
    - backend/app/curation/engine.py:328-343 — neighbours is a flat set of supporter fan_ids with no weight attached
    - team/memory/backlog.md — 'Mega-supporters flatten the signal ... Worth measuring before building'
    - frontend/src/features/feed/FeedCard.tsx:33 — the card can only show matched_tags today; there is no relationship to display because none is computed
